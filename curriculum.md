# HerCode 她编社 — Scratch Foundations

Student-led KAP curriculum · 12 weeks · intended session length: 50 minutes.

> Public resource: lesson outline and teaching activities only. Lesson slide decks, worksheets and student submissions have not been uploaded.

> Note: Week 1 and Week 2 agenda timings have been adjusted from the draft so each fits a 50-minute KAP session; activities and goals are otherwise preserved.

## Teaching approach

Learn through explanation, interactive examples, guided practice, individual projects, debugging, peer feedback and reflection.

## Weeks 1–4 — Explore

Learn the Scratch interface, movement, coordinates, sound and animation.

### Week 1: Introduction to Scratch

**Objective:** Become familiar with Scratch, the editor, and examples of what students can create.

**Agenda:**

- **Get to know one another (10 min):** Students and instructors introduce themselves and what they hope to make.
- **What is Scratch? (20 min):** Show beginner projects and tour the interface, including where blocks and the project stage are located.
- **Find it yourself (20 min):** Use a short interface scavenger hunt to locate key tools and practice navigating the editor.

### Week 2: Cats and their footprints

**Objective:** Use Move and Turn commands to create purposeful character movement.

**Agenda:**

- **Movement maze game (10 min):** Guide a volunteer through a physical maze by giving clear directions.
- **Meet the motion blocks (10 min):** Demonstrate Scratch Move and Turn blocks and connect them to instructions used in the game.
- **Help the cat find the fish (15 min):** Turn a character route into a sequence of Scratch commands.
- **Build a maze solution (15 min):** Students independently program a maze and ask for support when needed.

### Week 3: Moving around

**Objective:** Use the x–y coordinate plane to move sprites horizontally and vertically.

**Agenda:**

- **Coordinates in action (20 min):** Introduce the horizontal and vertical axes and practice identifying coordinates.
- **Code the movement (10 min):** Demonstrate coordinate-based movement in Scratch.
- **Try it yourself (20 min):** Move sprites left, right, up and down; then combine horizontal and vertical steps.

### Week 4: Sounds and animation

**Objective:** Combine graphics and recorded audio to create expressive interactive characters.

**Agenda:**

- **See a talking character (10 min):** Start with a button-triggered sound example.
- **Import visuals and sound (20 min):** Demonstrate how to add images and audio to a Scratch project.
- **Make a character speak (20 min):** Students record a short sound clip, import it, and connect it to an action.

## Weeks 5–6 — Logic

Introduce variables, broadcasting and connected game behaviors.

### Week 5: What are variables?

**Objective:** Understand variables as named values that can change during a program.

**Agenda:**

- **Variable basics (20 min):** Explore a named number and consider what values it could take.
- **Game-state brainstorm (30 min):** In small groups, use sticky notes to map variables such as position or score in a game.

### Week 6: How do I talk with my code?

**Objective:** Use broadcast and receive to coordinate actions and trigger events.

**Agenda:**

- **Investigate a snake game (20 min):** Ask how a game knows when to generate the next apple after one is collected.
- **Understand broadcast (10 min):** Model a Start button announcing that the game has begun.
- **Create a trigger (20 min):** Combine broadcasting with an earlier sound task and practice in small groups.

## Weeks 7–8 — Challenges

Use learned skills in maze puzzles and interactive storytelling.

### Week 7: Challenge: Can you complete the maze?

**Objective:** Apply coordinates and movement commands to solve a maze independently.

**Agenda:**

- **Quick review (5 min):** Revisit the x–y coordinate system.
- **Worked example (10 min):** Watch a sprite navigate a sample maze with code and coordinate annotations.
- **Individual challenge (30 min):** Solve a Scratch maze with optional hints and 1:1 support.
- **Show and tell (5 min):** Volunteers explain their maze solutions.

### Week 8: Challenge: Make a story!

**Objective:** Create a three-scene digital story using animation, audio, broadcasts, and movement.

**Agenda:**

- **Explore digital storytelling (15 min):** Analyze a simple animated story to identify scenes, dialogue and transitions.
- **Build a three-scene story (35 min):** Students plan and create an interactive story using previously learned techniques.

## Weeks 9–12 — Capstone

Design, build, debug and present an original Scratch game.

### Week 9: Project 1: Your game, your choice

**Objective:** Plan an original game, including characters, goals, logic and a realistic scope.

**Agenda:**

- **Explore ideas (15 min):** Study examples such as platformers, simulations, and quizzes.
- **Make a game-design plan (20 min):** Use worksheets to outline sprites, backgrounds, goals, and challenges.
- **Teacher check-in (15 min):** Review feasibility, adjust scope, and begin an initial prototype.

### Week 10: Project 2: Coding time!

**Objective:** Build the core game mechanics and progress toward a playable version.

**Agenda:**

- **Warm-up challenge (5 min):** Try a bouncing sprite or keyboard-controlled movement.
- **Main build session (30 min):** Code movement, interactions and basic win/loss logic.
- **Support and feedback (15 min):** Debug with teachers and practice peer problem-solving.

### Week 11: Project 3: Is your game complete?

**Objective:** Test, debug, and polish a complete project ready for presentation.

**Agenda:**

- **Polish checklist (10 min):** Review possible elements such as a title screen, restart, sounds and scoring.
- **Final code and testing (40 min):** Refine the game and gather peer feedback by playing and testing.

### Week 12: Project presentation time

**Objective:** Share original work, practice presenting and reflect on growth.

**Agenda:**

- **Play and present (40 min):** Students demonstrate their games and explore one another’s projects; parents may be invited if arrangements allow.
- **Celebrate and reflect (10 min):** Write an “I’m proud of…” reflection and celebrate course completion.
