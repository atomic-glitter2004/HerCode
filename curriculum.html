Scratch Foundations is a student-led, 12-week introduction to creative programming. Explore the goals and activities behind each class, from a student's first movement block to a game they can present.

Our teaching approach

HerCode pairs short explanations with interactive demonstrations, guided experiments, individual work, and frequent feedback. The final four meetings are dedicated to planning, building, testing and presenting an original game.

  1. Start with an example students recognize.
  2. Demonstrate a coding idea in context.
  3. Give students room to try it themselves.
  4. Use feedback, peer testing and reflection to improve.

What's included

All 12 lesson titles, learning objectives and session activity outlines from the HerCode student-led KAP plan.

MODULE 01 · EXPLORE

Weeks 1–4

Learn the Scratch interface, movement, coordinates, sound and animation.

01
WEEK 01 · EXPLORE

Introduction to Scratch

Become familiar with Scratch, the editor, and examples of what students can create.

+
Learning goalBecome familiar with Scratch, the editor, and examples of what students can create.

Session activities

  1. 10 min
    Get to know one another

    Students and instructors introduce themselves and what they hope to make.

  2. 20 min
    What is Scratch?

    Show beginner projects and tour the interface, including where blocks and the project stage are located.

  3. 20 min
    Find it yourself

    Use a short interface scavenger hunt to locate key tools and practice navigating the editor.

02
WEEK 02 · EXPLORE

Cats and their footprints

Use Move and Turn commands to create purposeful character movement.

+
Learning goalUse Move and Turn commands to create purposeful character movement.

Session activities

  1. 10 min
    Movement maze game

    Guide a volunteer through a physical maze by giving clear directions.

  2. 10 min
    Meet the motion blocks

    Demonstrate Scratch Move and Turn blocks and connect them to instructions used in the game.

  3. 15 min
    Help the cat find the fish

    Turn a character route into a sequence of Scratch commands.

  4. 15 min
    Build a maze solution

    Students independently program a maze and ask for support when needed.

03
WEEK 03 · EXPLORE

Moving around

Use the x–y coordinate plane to move sprites horizontally and vertically.

+
Learning goalUse the x–y coordinate plane to move sprites horizontally and vertically.

Session activities

  1. 20 min
    Coordinates in action

    Introduce the horizontal and vertical axes and practice identifying coordinates.

  2. 10 min
    Code the movement

    Demonstrate coordinate-based movement in Scratch.

  3. 20 min
    Try it yourself

    Move sprites left, right, up and down; then combine horizontal and vertical steps.

04
WEEK 04 · EXPLORE

Sounds and animation

Combine graphics and recorded audio to create expressive interactive characters.

+
Learning goalCombine graphics and recorded audio to create expressive interactive characters.

Session activities

  1. 10 min
    See a talking character

    Start with a button-triggered sound example.

  2. 20 min
    Import visuals and sound

    Demonstrate how to add images and audio to a Scratch project.

  3. 20 min
    Make a character speak

    Students record a short sound clip, import it, and connect it to an action.

MODULE 02 · LOGIC

Weeks 5–6

Introduce variables, broadcasting and connected game behaviors.

05
WEEK 05 · LOGIC

What are variables?

Understand variables as named values that can change during a program.

+
Learning goalUnderstand variables as named values that can change during a program.

Session activities

  1. 20 min
    Variable basics

    Explore a named number and consider what values it could take.

  2. 30 min
    Game-state brainstorm

    In small groups, use sticky notes to map variables such as position or score in a game.

06
WEEK 06 · LOGIC

How do I talk with my code?

Use broadcast and receive to coordinate actions and trigger events.

+
Learning goalUse broadcast and receive to coordinate actions and trigger events.

Session activities

  1. 20 min
    Investigate a snake game

    Ask how a game knows when to generate the next apple after one is collected.

  2. 10 min
    Understand broadcast

    Model a Start button announcing that the game has begun.

  3. 20 min
    Create a trigger

    Combine broadcasting with an earlier sound task and practice in small groups.

MODULE 03 · CHALLENGES

Weeks 7–8

Use learned skills in maze puzzles and interactive storytelling.

07
WEEK 07 · CHALLENGES

Challenge: Can you complete the maze?

Apply coordinates and movement commands to solve a maze independently.

+
Learning goalApply coordinates and movement commands to solve a maze independently.

Session activities

  1. 5 min
    Quick review

    Revisit the x–y coordinate system.

  2. 10 min
    Worked example

    Watch a sprite navigate a sample maze with code and coordinate annotations.

  3. 30 min
    Individual challenge

    Solve a Scratch maze with optional hints and 1:1 support.

  4. 5 min
    Show and tell

    Volunteers explain their maze solutions.

08
WEEK 08 · CHALLENGES

Challenge: Make a story!

Create a three-scene digital story using animation, audio, broadcasts, and movement.

+
Learning goalCreate a three-scene digital story using animation, audio, broadcasts, and movement.

Session activities

  1. 15 min
    Explore digital storytelling

    Analyze a simple animated story to identify scenes, dialogue and transitions.

  2. 35 min
    Build a three-scene story

    Students plan and create an interactive story using previously learned techniques.

MODULE 04 · CAPSTONE

Weeks 9–12

Design, build, debug and present an original Scratch game.

09
WEEK 09 · CAPSTONE

Project 1: Your game, your choice

Plan an original game, including characters, goals, logic and a realistic scope.

+
Learning goalPlan an original game, including characters, goals, logic and a realistic scope.

Session activities

  1. 15 min
    Explore ideas

    Study examples such as platformers, simulations, and quizzes.

  2. 20 min
    Make a game-design plan

    Use worksheets to outline sprites, backgrounds, goals, and challenges.

  3. 15 min
    Teacher check-in

    Review feasibility, adjust scope, and begin an initial prototype.

10
WEEK 10 · CAPSTONE

Project 2: Coding time!

Build the core game mechanics and progress toward a playable version.

+
Learning goalBuild the core game mechanics and progress toward a playable version.

Session activities

  1. 5 min
    Warm-up challenge

    Try a bouncing sprite or keyboard-controlled movement.

  2. 30 min
    Main build session

    Code movement, interactions and basic win/loss logic.

  3. 15 min
    Support and feedback

    Debug with teachers and practice peer problem-solving.

11
WEEK 11 · CAPSTONE

Project 3: Is your game complete?

Test, debug, and polish a complete project ready for presentation.

+
Learning goalTest, debug, and polish a complete project ready for presentation.

Session activities

  1. 10 min
    Polish checklist

    Review possible elements such as a title screen, restart, sounds and scoring.

  2. 40 min
    Final code and testing

    Refine the game and gather peer feedback by playing and testing.

12
WEEK 12 · CAPSTONE

Project presentation time

Share original work, practice presenting and reflect on growth.

+
Learning goalShare original work, practice presenting and reflect on growth.

Session activities

  1. 40 min
    Play and present

    Students demonstrate their games and explore one another’s projects; parents may be invited if arrangements allow.

  2. 10 min
    Celebrate and reflect

    Write an “I’m proud of…” reflection and celebrate course completion.
